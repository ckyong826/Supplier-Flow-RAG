# Eaton xPole PF7 residual-current-device catalogue reference

Source: Eaton, [xPole PF7 RCCB catalogue](https://www.eaton.com/content/dam/eaton/products/electrical-circuit-protection/circuit-breakers/xpole-rccb/eaton-xpole-pf7-rccb-catalog-ca019032en-en-us.pdf).

This reference digest covers the PF7 catalogue data visible for Eaton xPole residual-current devices. It is not a SupplierFlow stock or price record. Check the original Eaton catalogue for the full product row, national approval and installation requirements.

## PF7 product identity

PF7 is a residual-current-device family. The catalogue lists two-pole and four-pole configurations, Type AC variants and combinations of rated current and rated residual operating current. A PF7 reference should therefore be read as a complete combination, not as a family-level default.

The catalogue uses the notation `In/IΔn`, where `In` is the rated current and `IΔn` is the rated residual current. Those fields must remain separate in an answer. A 63 A device with 30 mA sensitivity is not equivalent to a 63 A device with 300 mA sensitivity.

## Rated-current options

The visible PF7 table includes 25 A, 40 A, 63 A, 80 A and 100 A rated-current options. Not every current appears in every pole or residual-current combination. The product row, not the family name, controls the answer.

The table also includes residual-current values of 0.03 A, 0.10 A, 0.30 A and 0.50 A. These correspond to 30 mA, 100 mA, 300 mA and 500 mA. A response should show both the catalogue notation and the converted unit if conversion helps the customer.

## Exact example: article 263584

The catalogue lists article number 263584 as a four-pole PF7 device with a rated-current/residual-current combination of 25/0.03. Its designation is PF7-25/4/003. In plain units, that is 25 A rated current and 30 mA rated residual operating current.

This example is useful because the number 263584 can be confused with nearby 63 A or 40 A four-pole entries. Pole count alone does not determine current. The article number, designation and table row must be kept together.

## Type AC boundary

The table section identifies the listed devices as Type AC and conditionally surge-current-proof 250 A in the shown category. This is a device-type statement. It does not mean the product is Type A, Type F or Type B.

If the customer’s load can produce pulsating DC or mixed-frequency residual currents, the assistant should not silently upgrade the PF7 Type AC description. Ask for the required residual-current type and retrieve a product family that explicitly supports it.

## Poles and packaging

Two-pole and four-pole devices are different forms. Four poles do not mean four independently protected overcurrent poles; an RCCB is not an MCB. The exact device symbol and terminal arrangement must be used for wiring.

The catalogue also includes units-per-package information. Packaging quantity is a logistics field, not an electrical rating. It should never be interpreted as the number of poles, the number of protected conductors or the number of devices installed.

## What the catalogue does not say

The product table does not establish current stock, local price, delivery lead time, warranty term or a compatible MCB for every installation. Those are separate operational or design questions. SupplierFlow must abstain when those fields are absent from its own knowledge base.

## Answering rule

For an exact PF7 article, return the designation, poles, rated current and residual current from the same row. For a family comparison, separate Type AC, rated current and IΔn. Do not infer 63 A from a four-pole article, and do not infer a different residual-current type from the sensitivity alone.
