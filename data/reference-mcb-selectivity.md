# MCB discrimination and selectivity reference

Reference facts for coordinating upstream and downstream circuit breakers.

- Discrimination, also called selectivity, is coordination so the protective device immediately upstream of the fault clears the fault while other healthy circuits remain energised.
- Total discrimination means this behaviour is maintained for the full prospective fault-current range covered by the installation and device combination.
- Partial discrimination means it is guaranteed only up to a declared selectivity limit; above that limit, the upstream device may also trip.
- Selectivity depends on the actual breaker pair, settings, fault level, and the manufacturer's coordination tables. Do not infer it from the breaker curve or kA rating alone.
