# TeSys Deca motor-starter catalogue

Source: Schneider Electric, [TeSys Catalog 2026](https://www.se.com/us/en/download/document/MKTED210011EN/), document MKTED210011EN.

This reference digest covers the structure of the TeSys catalogue. It is not a SupplierFlow quote, price list, stock record, delivery commitment or warranty statement. Exact contactor and accessory selection must use the current manufacturer table.

Quick answer: 1NO + 1NC auxiliary contacts alone do not prove a contactor's AC-3 motor rating or coil voltage; those fields require the exact product row. The catalogue lists TeSys Deca Contactors, TeSys Deca overload relays, GV2 and GV3 ranges.

## Catalogue scope

The catalogue groups ready-to-use motor starters and the components used to build a motor starter. Its related ranges include TeSys Deca contactors, GV2 and GV3 manual starters and protectors, TeSys Deca overload relays, control relays, TeSys F contactors, TeSys Giga equipment, motor-circuit protectors and TeSys island solutions.

This broad scope is why “TeSys” is not a sufficient product identity. A contactor, manual motor starter, overload relay and motor circuit breaker perform different jobs. The assistant must first identify the requested device type, then retrieve the matching family table.

## TeSys Deca contactors

Contactor selection normally uses the application category, motor or load current, operational voltage, coil-control voltage, number of poles, auxiliary contacts, mounting arrangement and required accessories. AC-1 and AC-3 are not interchangeable application labels. A contactor suitable for a resistive AC-1 duty may not have the same motor-switching rating in AC-3.

The coil voltage identifies the control circuit, not the load circuit. Two contactors with the same AC-3 current can have different coils and different auxiliary arrangements. A SKU comparison must keep the coil suffix and the power-contact rating separate.

## Motor-starter components

A motor starter usually needs a switching device and protection. The contactor controls the motor circuit. An overload relay protects against sustained overload. Short-circuit protection is provided by a suitable upstream protective device or motor-protection breaker. Accessories can provide auxiliary signalling, suppression, mechanical interlocking or connection support.

One component should not be described as performing every function of the complete starter. If the user asks for motor protection, ask whether they need overload protection, short-circuit protection, switching, isolation or all of them in an assembly.

## Advanced contactors and connection technology

The 2026 catalogue introduces TeSys Deca Advanced contactors and motor-starter solutions with SNAP IN connections up to 15 kW. A connection-technology feature is not the same as a current or breaking-capacity rating. It tells the installer how the conductors are connected and which product range supports that connection method.

The maximum motor power mentioned for a connection solution should not be copied to every contactor in the TeSys range. Motor power depends on voltage, efficiency, power factor, utilization category and duty. The exact table is still required.

## Auxiliary contacts

Auxiliary contacts are control and indication contacts. “1NO + 1NC” tells how many normally open and normally closed auxiliary paths are available; it does not by itself state the motor’s AC-3 power, the coil voltage or the short-circuit rating.

The terminal numbering and accessory compatibility depend on the product family. A customer comparing two similar LC1D references should receive a side-by-side table with the exact SKU, coil, main contacts, auxiliary contacts, utilization category and current rating.

## Manual starters and protectors

GV2 and GV3 manual starters and protectors are separate ranges in the catalogue. Their presence beside TeSys Deca contactors does not mean that a GV2 or GV3 reference is a contactor replacement. Manual motor protection includes a different operating mechanism and adjustment range.

For a complete starter, verify the contactor, overload relay or motor-protection device, auxiliary block, enclosure and connection accessories as a compatible set. A catalogue family overview alone cannot prove compatibility.

## Safe catalogue answer

When the customer provides only a motor power, do not jump directly to a SKU. Ask for voltage, phase, full-load current, starting method, duty, coil/control voltage and protection requirements. When the customer provides an exact SKU, retrieve the exact product row and avoid adding fields from a sibling reference.

The catalogue is strong evidence for product-family structure and selection fields. It is not evidence for SupplierFlow price, current stock, lead time or quotation validity; those remain operational facts that must come from the business system or sales team.
