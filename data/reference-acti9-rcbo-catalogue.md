# Acti9 iC60N3 and iC60H3 RCBO catalogue

Source: Schneider Electric, [Acti9 iC60N3/H3 RCBOs Catalog](https://www.se.com/uk/en/download/document/CA902086E/), document CA902086E.

This reference digest covers the manufacturer’s compact three-phase RCBO catalogue. It does not add SupplierFlow pricing, stock, quotation, delivery, or warranty information. Use the original catalogue for the exact commercial reference and local approval.

## What these devices are

The catalogue covers Acti9 iC60N3 and iC60H3 compact three-phase RCBOs. An RCBO combines overcurrent protection and residual-current protection in one device. That combination is different from an RCCB, which requires separate overcurrent protection. The word “RCBO” should therefore be retained when describing this family.

These products are intended to simplify professional distribution-board installation. The catalogue highlights compatibility with the Acti9 system, reduced wiring connections, selectivity and cascading options, and accessories such as padlocking devices. Those system features do not remove the need to check the installation diagram.

## Main performance distinction

The iC60N3 family is listed with a 6000 A, or 6 kA, breaking capacity. The iC60H3 family is listed with a 10000 A, or 10 kA, breaking capacity. These are not two spellings for one value. A comparison that says both devices have the same short-circuit capacity is wrong unless the exact table says so.

Breaking capacity must be read with the voltage, standard and pole arrangement. “10 kA” is not a general statement that applies at every voltage or for every wiring condition. The answer should preserve the unit and the family name, and should not turn a catalogue entry into an installation guarantee.

## Current and residual-current fields

The catalogue lists ratings from 6 A to 50 A for the covered compact RCBO range. Residual-current sensitivities include 10 mA, 30 mA and 100 mA options. The rated current and residual-current sensitivity answer different questions: one concerns the overcurrent path, while the other concerns the residual-current trip threshold.

A 30 mA sensitivity should not be silently replaced with 100 mA, and a 6 A product should not be represented as a 16 A product because both belong to the same family. When comparing two references, show current, sensitivity, poles, curve, breaking capacity and device family as separate rows.

## Protection claim boundaries

The catalogue describes protection against short circuits, cable overloads and electric shock for the covered RCBO products. That describes the intended protective functions of the device. It does not prove that every installation is safe, selective or compliant. Cable sizing, prospective fault current, earthing system, disconnection time and local code still require engineering verification.

An RCBO is not the same as an RCCB plus any arbitrary MCB. The combined device has its own thermal, magnetic and residual-current characteristics, terminals and accessories. If the user asks for a substitute, identify the required standard, poles, curve, current, residual-current type and breaking capacity before suggesting one.

## Monitoring and accessories

The catalogue mentions connectivity with PowerTag Energy Sensors, Universal Panel Server and EcoStruxure Energy Hub for remote monitoring. These communication or energy-monitoring features are complementary. They should not be described as changing the fundamental trip rating of the RCBO.

Padlocking and other accessories also need exact compatibility checks. An accessory that fits one Acti9 family may not fit another family with a similar appearance. Product questions should therefore retrieve the product table and accessory table together when the user asks for a complete assembly.

## Selection workflow

First identify whether the user needs an RCBO, RCCB or MCB. Next capture the number of phases and poles. Then capture rated current, tripping curve, residual-current sensitivity and type, breaking capacity, voltage and standard. Only after those fields are known should the assistant compare the iC60N3 and iC60H3 families.

If one requested field is absent, answer the fields that are supported and explicitly abstain for the missing field. Do not infer a sensitivity from the product family, and do not infer a breaking capacity from the letter N or H without the catalogue table.
