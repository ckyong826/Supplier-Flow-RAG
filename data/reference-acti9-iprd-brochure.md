Source: Schneider Electric, [Acti9 & Resi9 new SPD Digital Brochure](https://www.se.com/uk/en/download/document/Acti9-Resi9_Brochure/).

This reference digest summarizes the manufacturer brochure at a selection level. It does not create a local SupplierFlow price, stock, delivery, warranty or installation approval. The original brochure and the applicable national rules remain authoritative.

Quick answer: a higher SPD kA number alone is not enough; check the network system, nominal voltage, earthing arrangement and coordination.

## Two protection roles

The brochure distinguishes the Acti9 iPRD1 family from the Acti9 iPRD family. The “1” in iPRD1 is associated with Type 1 surge-protection applications, while iPRD is used for Type 2 protection applications. The type describes the device’s role and test class; it is not simply a product size or a marketing grade.

Type 1 and Type 2 devices are selected according to the lightning-risk and installation arrangement. A Type 1 device may be required where a lightning-current component can enter the installation, while Type 2 protection is commonly used in distribution boards against induced or conducted transients. The design must determine the required arrangement.

## Do not select on kA alone

An SPD cannot be selected safely from a single discharge-current number. The installer must check the network system, nominal operating voltage, number of poles or protected conductors, earthing arrangement, backup protection, connection length, coordination with upstream and downstream SPDs, and the equipment being protected.

Two devices may both display a kA value but have different type, voltage and wiring requirements. They are not interchangeable merely because the number looks larger. A larger headline rating can still be the wrong device for the system.

## Installation position

The brochure’s purpose is coordinated surge protection. In practice, the SPD’s position in the installation is part of its performance. Long conductors add inductive voltage during a surge, so the connection route, protective conductor and bonding must be kept within the manufacturer’s rules. A downstream Type 3 device may be needed when sensitive equipment is remote from the distribution-board Type 2 device.

The assistant should never turn a general brochure statement into a fixed “safe distance” without the exact source rule. If the user asks whether a Type 2 device alone is enough, the answer should explain that protection depends on installation category, wiring length, equipment sensitivity and coordination.

## Product family versus cartridge

The iPRD and iPRD1 families can include complete products, accessories and replacement cartridges. A cartridge reference is for maintenance of a compatible device. It should not be returned as if it were the complete SPD unless the customer explicitly asks for the spare.

The product name, type, voltage and pole arrangement should be preserved when an answer is generated. “iPRD” alone is too broad for a purchase recommendation. The same family name can cover multiple network configurations and discharge-current ratings.

## Status and maintenance

Surge arresters have an end-of-life state that may be shown locally or through an auxiliary contact, depending on the product. End-of-life indication tells maintenance that the protective element needs attention; it does not guarantee that the protected load is still energised or that a surge did not already damage equipment.

Replacing a cartridge should follow the product instruction. Isolate the circuit, confirm the exact spare reference and preserve the wiring arrangement. Never infer compatibility from physical appearance alone.

## Practical response pattern

For a customer asking “which SPD should I buy?”, first classify the requested protection role: Type 1, Type 2 or local Type 3. Then ask for system voltage, earthing system, poles, installation location and equipment sensitivity. If the catalogue does not provide a local commercial field, answer the technical match and route price or availability to sales.

The brochure supports product-family understanding. It is not a substitute for the complete installation design, the original technical table or a local electrical professional’s verification.
