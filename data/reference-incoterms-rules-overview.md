# Incoterms 2020 rules overview for supplier conversations

Source: International Chamber of Commerce, [Incoterms 2020 rules and official Q&A](https://library.iccwbo.org/clp/clp-incoterms-qa-2020.htm?AGENT=ICC_HQ).

This reference digest explains what an Incoterms rule can and cannot tell a supplier or customer. It is not SupplierFlow payment, tax, stock or delivery policy. A commercial quotation still needs its own price, timing, tax and service terms.

## What Incoterms does

Incoterms 2020 allocates responsibilities between seller and buyer. The selected rule identifies delivery obligations, the point at which risk transfers, which party arranges carriage, and how certain costs or insurance obligations are allocated. The named place is part of the rule and must be written accurately.

These are contract-allocation concepts. They do not replace the sales contract, purchase order, transport booking, customs documents or local law. The assistant should explain the rule’s scope without treating it as a complete quotation.

## Delivery is not the same as arrival

The ICC Q&A distinguishes a delivery point from a destination to which carriage is paid. In a C rule, the seller may arrange and pay for carriage to the named destination while risk transfers earlier, for example when goods are handed to the carrier. Therefore “delivered to Singapore” can be ambiguous unless the rule and named place are written precisely.

A destination named for carriage does not automatically mean that the goods are risk-free until arrival. The risk-transfer event comes from the selected rule and the required delivery action. The assistant must not collapse carriage, risk and arrival into one event.

## Costs and risk

Incoterms helps allocate carriage, export/import formalities, insurance under the relevant rule and other listed costs. The parties still need to define taxes, duties, payment timing, inspection, packaging, documents and service-level commitments elsewhere if the rule does not settle them.

If a customer asks “who pays?”, the answer should name the rule and cost category. If the question asks for a shipping charge, the rule alone is insufficient because the carrier, route, weight, customs and quotation date affect the amount.

## C rules

Under a C rule, the seller commonly arranges carriage to the named destination, but delivery and risk can occur at an earlier point. CPT and CIP are examples where risk can transfer when the goods are handed to the carrier even though the seller pays carriage to the named destination. Insurance obligations differ between rules.

The word “CIP” alone does not state a transit time, stock availability or tax rate. It identifies a contract framework. A Singapore transit estimate requires a route, carrier, shipment data and confirmed operational schedule.

## D rules

D rules move the seller’s delivery obligation closer to the named destination, but the exact rule still determines unloading, import clearance and risk details. DAP, DPU and DDP should not be treated as identical. The named place and clearance responsibility must be read with the rule.

Even a D rule does not promise a number of working days. A delivery estimate is a separate commercial or logistics statement. The assistant should abstain when the knowledge base does not publish the requested route time.

## What it does not answer

An Incoterms rule does not by itself provide product price, payment term, SST rate, credit limit, available stock, carrier transit time or a guaranteed delivery date. It does not select the correct product or prove that a destination is serviceable.

## Response pattern

When a customer asks about an Incoterms term, identify the buyer/seller allocation, named place, risk-transfer event and carriage obligation. Then state the missing commercial fields. Do not answer a delivery-time question from the Incoterms word alone.
